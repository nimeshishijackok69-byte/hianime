## The Whisper + Kaggle/Colab Question: Can It Do 2-Second Chunks?

**Short answer: Whisper was NOT architected for this, but there are workarounds.**

### Why Whisper Struggles with Short Chunks

Whisper's encoder is hardcoded to a **30-second mel-spectrogram window** . When you feed it 2 seconds of audio, it zero-pads the remaining 28 seconds and still runs the full encoder forward pass — wasting ~90% of compute . This is why real-time Whisper implementations typically show **3-5 second latency** even on good hardware .

Research from the *Whisper-Streaming* paper confirms this: with 1-second minimum chunk sizes, average latency is **3.3 seconds for English**, and quality drops 2-6% WER compared to offline mode .

### What People Actually Do

The standard approach is a **sliding window with overlap** — not true 2-second chunks :

- Capture audio continuously
- Process overlapping 4-8 second segments
- Stitch transcripts together with deduplication logic
- Accept 2-5 second latency as the cost of using Whisper

This is what `whisper.cpp` streaming examples and `faster-whisper` projects implement, but it introduces stitching errors and accumulating latency .

### Can You Force 2-Second Chunks?

Technically yes, practically no:

- You can pad 2 seconds to 30 seconds and run inference, but latency will be **worse** than using larger chunks because you're paying the full encoder cost for almost no audio 
- Without sufficient context, Whisper hallucinates and produces garbage text
- The academic research specifically warns against this: "larger MinChunkSize, the larger the latency, but higher the quality"
